About

How I got here.

I started on factory floors. At Alpha Measurement I pulled usage history out of the warehouse ERP, ranked every part by how often it moved, and put the fastest movers closer to the pick face. Average pick time dropped 25%. At Tesla I built material flow models and warehouse layouts across three production shops, and identified millions in savings in dock allocation and storage. At Boeing I keep an eight-person engineering program on schedule as Scrum Master, and built the data systems that cut executive reporting time by two thirds.

That is the whole pattern. Find the thing being done by hand, build what replaces it, measure what changed. Industrial engineering is how I think about it: inputs, constraints, outcomes. It is also why the systems I build are ones I trust to run on their own.

Outside work I build things at home to learn. From March to June 2026 I spent nights and weekends on Soltreya, a solo side project: a quality platform for manufacturers on a multi-agent framework I wrote from scratch. It never had customers and I stopped building it in June. Now I am building Holon, a personal operating system I use every day, where I work on the parts of AI systems that are hardest to get right: approval gates, retrieval, and cost limits.

The WAT framework

Workflows, Agents, Tools. The reliability pattern under everything: probabilistic AI handles reasoning while deterministic code handles execution, so 90%-accurate steps never compound into failure.
